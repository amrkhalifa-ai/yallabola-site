2. What the app is

It also helps people arrange those games: publishing a bola, asking for a seat, approving somebody, and confirming afterwards who turned up. The meeting itself happens between you and the other people, in the physical world, and we are not part of it. See section 7.

Deciding to meet someone, to give them your address, or to go to theirs, is your decision and yours alone. Meet in a place you are comfortable with. Tell somebody where you are going. We provide the introduction and nothing more, and we are not responsible for what happens when you meet.

Hosts: giving an address to confirmed players is a choice you make each time. Players: an address given to you is given for one bola.

8. Ratings, results and no-shows

A rated bola produces a result the players confirm, and it moves a rating. A player who disagrees can dispute it, which freezes the result until a moderator decides.

A host can record that somebody did not turn up. An accusation is not a verdict: it is held until it is corroborated or the person accused has had the chance to answer it, and only then does it appear on a show-up record. Making a false accusation is a breach of section 4.

Ratings and records are ours to calculate and to correct. We may void a result obtained dishonestly, and remove the rating changes that came from it.

9. About the scores

These games are played to house rules that differ from table to table, town to town and family to family. The app computes scores according to the ruleset you configure, and it stores a frozen copy of those rules with each game so that later changes never rewrite an old result.

The app is a convenience for the people at the table. It is not an authority, and it does not settle arguments. If the app and the table disagree, the table is right. We are not responsible for the outcome of any game, or for anything wagered on one — which, per section 4, should be nothing.
